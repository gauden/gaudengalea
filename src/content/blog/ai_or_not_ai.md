“Fear is not a strategy.”

That line became the centre of a short video I made for a session on artificial intelligence and public health. Rather than give a conventional talk, I staged a conversation between two fictional AI agents: **Dystostrich**, who sees the risks of AI arriving faster than governance, and **Owltopia**, who sees the practical value of careful, responsible adoption.

Dystostrich worries about energy use, misinformation, cyber-risk, degraded evidence, and platform power. Owltopia argues that AI can help public health with surveillance, synthesis, early warning, triage, and better use of scarce human time.

The point is not that one side wins. The point is that public health has to engage with both sides seriously. Panic is not enough. Hype is not enough. Withdrawal leaves the field to others. Engagement, if disciplined and well governed, gives us a chance to shape the tools before they shape us.

The video first screened at **Plenum II: “Künstliche Intelligenz für die Öffentliche Gesundheit”** during the **75th Scientific Congress of the Öffentlicher Gesundheitsdienst**, held in Wuppertal, Germany, 20-23 April 2026.
